# About

Hi, my name is Alex and I work as a software developer in Toronto, Canada. This is my personal website, where I share some of my projects and passions. I was born in Bulgaria but I've spent most of my life in Canada. I finished a BSc in Mathematics at Simon Fraser University (2013) and an MSc in Mathematics at the University of British Columbia (2016). Since then I've been building a career in tech.

Ever since I was little, I've been drawn to the interplay between the abstract world of science/thought and the concrete world of physical movement. There is a lot of synergy between those worlds and I find it best when I participate in both.

My aim with this website is to showcase some of the projects I'm most proud of, as well as some athletics. You can think of this as my personal calling card. The website itself is a work in progress, so do come back from time to time to see how it evolves.

## Abstract

### Mathematics

Given that mathematics has fascinated members of my family for generations, it was probably a surprise to no one that I picked up math in school and later at university. What began as curiosity grew into a passion, and I set out to pursue a full academic career in the subject before shifting to software during grad school. Here are three mathematical projects that I am most proud of.

- [Multidimensional Lattice Walk Enumeration (Master's thesis)](/math/lattice-walks)
- [Linear Operators in the Theory of Electromagnetism (Undergraduate research project)](/math/electromagnetic-operators)
- [Scaling of Physical Processes in People (Independent research)](/math/scaling)

### Software

I first picked up software development in 2010 as a practical tool, during my first undergraduate research term (funded by a summer research award at SFU). The code I wrote helped me find an interesting combinatorial identity for spanning forest polynomials. From then on I wrote more and more code, especially while in grad school, and that allowed me to shift into a career in software development. You can try a couple of my interactive demos here: [A\* Algorithm](/demos/a-star) and [Perlin Noise Terrain](/demos/perlin-noise).

## Concrete

### Parkour

One of the most consequential moments of my life happened in the summer of 2004 when I discovered Parkour through a TV program. Over the next seven years I dedicated much of my life outside of school to the pursuit of mastery in movement. This practice showed me that I could achieve things I previously thought were impossible for me, and that shaped much of how I view myself and the world at large.

However, injuries took their toll, and eventually I had to stop training at a serious level. After I picked up ballroom dancing as a passion, I reduced Parkour to occasional sessions that helped me retain some of the moves and skills I had acquired over the years.

### Ballroom Dancing

After a number of injuries during my Parkour career, I needed a lower-impact activity that was still challenging enough to be enjoyable. When I picked up ballroom dancing at the UBC Dance Club in 2010, I had no idea how much I'd dedicate myself to the art and craft of this sport.

Over the next decade I grew through the different levels of International Standard and Latin and participated in over 50 competitions at home and abroad. The journey reached its peak when I placed second in Canada in the 30+ category of International Standard, at the 2019 Canadian Closed Championships in Calgary.
